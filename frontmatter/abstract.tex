\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

% Word count checked against guideline 4.1.6 (120-150 words recommended);

Autonomous mobile robots are typically developed in simulation before
deployment on physical hardware, yet the transfer to a real platform
routinely exposes gaps that simulated testing alone does not predict.
This thesis documents the migration of a ROS~2 Nav2 navigation stack
for the Quanser QCar, an Ackermann-steered vehicle, from Gazebo
simulation onto physical hardware. An incompatibility between the
robot's onboard ROS~2 Dashing distribution and the ROS~2 Humble
development host motivated a custom TCP/JSON bridge. The thesis
presents the bridge's design, the calibration of an open-loop throttle
model against real actuator behaviour, and the diagnosis of a hardware
buffering defect misattributed at first to drivetrain coasting. The
resulting system is validated through closed-loop Nav2 goal-reaching
tests on the vehicle, and the observed sim-to-real discrepancies are
quantified.

\noindent\textbf{Key words:} ROS~2, Nav2, Ackermann steering, sim-to-real
transfer, Gazebo, Model Predictive Path Integral control, mobile robot
navigation, Quanser QCar
